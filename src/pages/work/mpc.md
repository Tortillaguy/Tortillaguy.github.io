I joined MPC (Moving Picture Company) in February 2018 as a VR engineer, on a team of fewer than ten. Most of my year there went into two projects: Spotify for Magic Leap, and an AR app for National Geographic. I led a group of three developers and worked directly with Magic Leap's own team.

## Spotify for Magic Leap

Spotify's audio came in through a C++ plugin, and getting it to work meant extending Magic Leap's Lumin SDK in C++. On top of that I built the Unity app that wrapped the plugin and streamed music through it. Unity held all of the interface: album art pinned to the walls, the player and its controls floating in the room, and the custom shaders and music visualizations around them.

I left MPC in February 2019. The app [shipped on Magic Leap World on 13 November 2019](https://www.engadget.com/2019-11-13-spotify-magic-leap-ar-app.html), the first app to use Magic Leap's background music service, so music kept playing while you used other apps.

## National Geographic

The other project was an AR app for National Geographic that ran on phones and tablets. It placed a dinosaur on a real surface in front of you and worked as an interactive guide to its anatomy. You could explore the parts of the dinosaur, with audio cues and timelines explaining each one.

## Pipelines

I also wrote Python automation for MPC's VFX departments, moving assets between Maya, Nuke and the in-house renderers. It cut manual file handling by 50%.
